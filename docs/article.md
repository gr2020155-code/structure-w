## 1. Energy

Energy is the rate at which a particle lags behind its global figure-eight; E = γmc², E = ħω and p = ħk follow from this.

**Definition** \[author, A-60; reading — derived, V-211\]. The rate in A-60 is the exchange rate (exchanges per tick), not a velocity in space. Read as a velocity in space, energy would drop to zero as v → c, whereas in the data it grows.

**Why it is a difference and not a count of acts** \[derived, V-211\]. The points of a form change every tick (A-58), so almost all acts of a form are new pairs, as in the medium. A form differs from the medium only by a small difference in rate. For the electron this is one part in N, where N\_e = 2.39·10²² at c\_g = 1 (V-271); strictly from data N ≥ 3.7·10⁸ (V-19, LEP bound; if the layer rule is the same for charge and gravity, at least 2.04·10²¹, section 4).

**Derivation of E = γmc²** \[derived, V-213\]. Lag is a quantity counted in places of the medium. It can be counted in two ways.

1. Along the particle itself — at the rate of its clock m·ι, where ι is the closure fraction of a step (A-39). This is m/γ — time dilation, not energy.
2. At a fixed place in the medium, where the exchanges of the global figure-eight run — this is the energy E of A-60.
3. The difference in lag between neighbouring places is the momentum p. By A-39 a fraction β goes to transfer, so p = βE.
4. Per tick the particle shifts by β places. The count (addition only): along the particle = at the place − β × difference between places.

```latex
m\iota = E - \beta p = E(1-\beta^2) = E\iota^2 \;\Rightarrow\; E = \frac{m}{\iota} = \gamma m,\quad p = \gamma\beta m,\quad E^2 - p^2 = m^2
```

Numerical check: at β = 0.6 the fraction ι = 0.8 and E = 1.25m = γm; the same at β = 0.1, 0.9, 0.99. The clock and energy are one lag, counted along the particle and at a place.

**Consequence** \[derived, V-213\]. The formulas E = ħω and p = ħk, previously taken as a borrowing (A-04), follow from A-60. ω is the beat frequency of the pattern with the global figure-eight at a place, k is the difference in lag per step, ħ is the counting unit; its numerical value is measured.

**Particle collisions** \[derived, V-209\]. In an electron–positron collision at LEP (91.2 GeV) the transfer is 89 000 times larger than the closures. On impact the transfer is quenched and becomes the closures of new forms. The products are not parts of the electron: out come muon, tau and quark pairs and neutrinos of all three kinds. A νν̄ pair is figure-eights contracted to a point (A-18), that is, a rearrangement, not a break-up into parts.

**Not shown** \[open\]: that in a closed system it is precisely the sum of lags that is conserved.

## 2. How α shows up in the atom

In the atom α shows up as the fraction of a step that the light part spends on transfer from one side of the heavy part to the other, rather than on its own closure \[derived, V-169\]. The source of α is not the atom but the charge of a single particle (section 3, V-228): the transfer fraction is the product of the contributions of two charges, √α·√α.

**The atom is one configuration, not an electron in orbit** \[derived, V-168, V-175\].

- The light part (+, −) attaches with its (−)-loop to the (+)-end of the heavy part (+, −, +): directions alternate.
- Passing straight through is impossible: the (−)-loop would end up next to the (−)-middle — a contradiction.
- The ends of the heavy part are indistinguishable (A-31), so the light part must visit both: the transition is a figure-eight of two loops around.
- A link is an exchange of points (A-61): without dynamics there is no link, so the transition is necessary (V-215).

**Why α² and why /2** \[derived, V-169\]. The step budget is divided by squares: β² + ι² = 1. α² is the transfer share in this division. Closures run at the rate ι = √(1 − α²) ≈ 1 − α²/2: the square root gives the half.

**Binding energy, lag and the common act are one phenomenon** \[derived, V-158\]. With every transition the light part lags by one of its own cycles, and this cycle becomes common with the heavy part: one common cycle per ≈ 37 557 own cycles (2/α²). Binding energy is the same notion of lag as rest energy, one level up \[candidate, V-212\]. The number 37 557 comes from the measured α — a translation of data into the language of W, not an independent check.

**The atom system** \[derived, V-170\].

| No. | Equation | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | β² + ι² = 1 | A-39 |
| 2 | K(1 − ι) = 1 — one common cycle per transition | A-45 |
| 3 | L = βKN/ι — transfer steps per transition | A-01 |
| 4 | a cycle is a loop, a transition is a figure-eight of two loops | A-24 |

Solution: the atom size in orbit radii ≈ 1/β, binding energy ≈ β²/2, one common cycle per transition. At β = α the size comes out as 137.038 against the measured 137.036. The factor 2 comes from the shape of the transition: without a figure-eight of two loops the atom would be twice as large.

**The number 2 in P = 2/α** \[computed, V-214\]. Over a transition the lag phase is exactly 2 cycles, one per loop: the ground state is one cycle per loop. The transition path is 274.07 orbits, 137.04 per loop. The transition does not create this P, it inherits it from a single charge: by the data, the field energy of a charge outside the orbit radius is (α/2)·mc², i.e. the medium carries a fraction α/2 of the particle's lag, and one unit of lag goes into the far field over 2/α ≈ 274 orbits \[candidate, V-233\].

**Where the π in the anomaly α/2π comes from** \[candidate, V-240; derived, V-241\]. In the anomaly the electron's orbit closes: 2π is the circumference of the orbit radius taken as unity (A-66). α is the fraction of the charge's coupling to itself per full turn, a\_e = α/2π is the same per radian. The electron's orbit is 2.39·10²² steps at c\_g = 1 (V-271), strictly from data no less than 3.7·10⁸ (V-19); its closure operator is indistinguishable from π, as the data require. If a local loop of radius one step closed instead (operator ≈ 2.93), the first term of the anomaly would be 0.001245 instead of 0.001161 (+7 %) — excluded. This relation does not set the value of α; the coincidence 4π³ + π² + π = 137.03630 as an exact formula is excluded: it exceeds the measured value by 2.2·10⁻⁶, 10⁴ times the uncertainty.

**α and distance** \[candidate, V-242, V-244; derived, V-243\]. As in gravity the observable is the product GM while G is a unit conversion, so for charge what is constant is not α(L) but its product with a closure factor (A-68): α(L) = q²·(π/π\_c(L))^k, where π\_c(L) is the closure operator of the path between the charges. The direction is right: closure of a shorter path is less than π, so α is larger at short distances (1/137 in the atom, ≈ 1/128 at the Z boson). The law π\_c(L) and the exponent k were not derived: the simplest law, "a polygon with the number of sides ∝ distance", fitted to Z, gives 1/136.9 at 10 GeV against 131 in the data, where the growth is gradual, logarithmic, with kinks at the masses of charged particles. Closure depends on the path between the charges, not on the size of each particle: if a particle's α were set by the closure of its own orbit in the path units needed for 1/128 at Z, the charges of the proton and electron would differ by ~4·10⁻⁶, while the data give less than 10⁻²¹. The earlier explanation via layers of the medium (V-208) does not apply inside the atom (A-62). 25.11: the mechanism is refined — the change of α with distance is the screening of charge pushes in the medium between the charges (A-81, V-272); at the source a fraction q₀ ≈ 0.123 remains — the constant component of the alternating side (A-70, V-293, V-295); only pushes with a side are screened, so G is constant (section 9).

**Charge and magnetism are one loop** \[candidate, V-231\]. A loop is a circular exchange, i.e. a circular current, a small magnet; its orientation (+/−) is the sign of the charge; there are exactly two orientations — charge is quantised. The rule "neighbouring loops of the same direction are a contradiction" is like neighbouring gears: with the same direction the motion at the contact point is opposed. Direction of search: α as the ratio of the loop's outward coupling (charge) to its circular rotation (the magnetic side) — by analogy with e/g = 2α in Dirac's theory. Not derived.

**Magnetic moments of the proton and neutron** \[computed, V-253; candidate, V-254; derived, V-255; open, V-256\]. The rules were written before comparison with data. If the form is a single orbit of its own charge, then μ\_p = 1 and μ\_n = 0 nuclear magnetons against 2.793 and −1.913. So nucleons have internal currents the size of the nucleon itself (0.4–0.8 fm at speed c), 10⁴ times larger than the W core. The reading "the current comes from the transition of a charged closure element" agrees with the data for leptons and the proton: in leptons closure is a point, the anomaly is of order α (almost the same for electron and muon); in the proton closure is a loop, the anomaly is of order 1. But for the neutron it gives +1.79 instead of −1.91 — a sign contradiction. The ratio μ\_n/μ\_p = −0.685 ≈ −2/3 is a factor of spin addition of the parts; W has no rule for adding the spins of figure-eights, so it cannot be derived yet.

**Where the search stopped (18.11)** \[derived, V-251, V-260\]. The atom transition, the level loop, the junction and sides, fractions of directions, small integers, ticks of a single loop, alternation — each path was either excluded by calculation (section 7) or reduced to the same fraction q. The check of 18.11 also closed the remaining classes: the global figure-eight is not unique and does not fix the density of returns (section 4), and the orientation of loops does not set the side of an act \[computed, V-259\]. So q could not be derived from the postulates of that time. A new postulate was needed: either a selection rule making the trajectory unique (A-42), or a rule linking the constant part of the side to a level-2 number (orbits, transitions). Section 9 describes how this was resolved on 25.11.

## 4. Charge and gravity: one mechanism

Charge and gravity are one mechanism: the rule of transmission through layers is the same, only the sources differ \[author, A-69; derived, V-245\]. Hence the 1/r law for charge, the hierarchy of forces and a bound on the step of space — no more than 74 Planck lengths.

**The 1/r law for charge** \[derived, V-234\]. Continuations from a charge pass through each layer whole and are divided equally among its cells (A-64); the number of cells in a layer ∝ r², so the field ∝ 1/r², the potential ∝ 1/r — the same chain that gave the law of gravity (V-109…V-113). The energy of a cell is the square of its share (A-39), so the field energy outside radius R ∝ 1/R, as in the data (e²/2R). The coefficient is not derived — just like G.

**Hierarchy of forces** \[derived, V-245; candidate, V-235\]. Acts are indistinguishable (A-31), so the layer rule is the same, and for two electrons electric / gravitational = (q·N\_e)² — without the constants of the rule. By the data this is 4.2·10⁴² (for two protons 1.24·10³⁶), i.e. q·N\_e = 2.04·10²¹. Gravity is weaker because the electron closes once per ~10²² ticks, while an unscreened outward exchange happens once per ~12 (at c\_g = 1).

**α and G in one formula** \[candidate, V-269\]. From Gm²/ħc = c\_g/N² and m = h/(cNa) the product m·N = h/(ca) is the same for all particles, so G = c\_g·c³a²/(4π²ħ): G is set by the step a and the coupling c\_g alone, masses drop out. Together with α = c\_g·q²: α/G = 4π²q²·ħ/(c³a²), and in W units (a = c = ħ = 1) α/G = 4π²q². G cannot be expressed through α alone until one more number is derived: q (or, equivalently, the step a in units where α is given).

**The step of space and the Planck length** \[derived, V-252\]. The electron's figure-eight has exactly one free end at every tick, so q ≤ 1, N\_e ≥ 2.04·10²¹ and the step a = λ\_C/N\_e ≤ 1.19·10⁻³³ m = 74 l\_P — about 6·10¹² times tighter than the LEP bound. If the unit of coupling is the same for both mechanisms (c\_g = 1), then α = q², q = 1/11.706, N\_e = m\_P/m\_e = 2.39·10²² and the step a = 2π·l\_P = 1.016·10⁻³⁴ m \[candidate, V-235\]. The Planck formula l\_P = √(ħG/c³) was not put in: the step comes from the rules of W (mass is 1/N closures per tick, the source of gravity is closures) and the measured G. So it is independent of the Planck formula but not of G; the same step for e, μ, p is a consistency of W, not a separate check.

**Mass is a property of the global arrangement, charge is local** \[derived, V-247\]. With a random choice of partner a free end would return to the same partner in 1/14.8 of cases (R-50); a return of the same pair is a repetition, i.e. lag, and N would be ≈ 15 — a mass of Planck order. In the electron returns are suppressed by a factor of 1.6·10²¹; local statistics cannot give such suppression — only the order of the unique trajectory (A-42) with an arrow of time without return (A-40) can. Charge, on the contrary, is almost random: with a random side the fraction of unscreened exchanges is 0.105 (R-51), the required one is 0.085. So charge is a local property of the free end (hence the same in the electron and the proton) with a correction from order, while mass is a property of the global arrangement (V-162).

**Global figure-eights on small systems** \[computed, V-257, V-258\]. The conditions were written before the run: over a period of 2n ticks each point passes twice through O along two loops of opposite orientation, at every tick all points are split into exchange pairs, there is no return in the medium. On clouds of 8 places without a lattice such placements exist, but there are several: 3–6 per cloud (full enumeration), and with equal loops 0 or 10–11. They do not fix the density of returns: both 0 and at least 0.28 repetitions per point-tick are possible (random matchings of the same graph give 0.31). So the uniqueness of the trajectory and the suppression of returns (mass) do not follow from these postulates — a trajectory selection rule is needed. At the same time the points behave almost indistinguishably: in 14 solutions out of 17 all points have the same pair of loop lengths. The same at n = 10 (at least three solutions in each of two clouds) and in 3D (with equal loops — at least three solutions in each of three clouds).

**Prediction** \[derived, V-272; withdrawn 25.11\]. The earlier prediction — G changes together with α below ~4·10⁻¹³ m — is excluded: an opposing pair screens only pushes with a side, and closures (mass) have no side. G is constant, while the ratio α/G changes with distance; there is no longer a difference from standard physics here.

**System of constants** \[candidate, V-239\]. Through three W numbers — q (unscreened outward exchanges per tick), N\_e (ticks per electron orbit), c\_g (coupling of a unit flux through layers) — and the rules of forms, about ten constants are expressed:

```latex
\alpha = c_g\,q^2,\qquad \frac{G m_e^2}{\hbar c} = \frac{c_g}{N_e^2},\qquad a = \frac{\lambda_C}{N_e},\qquad \frac{a_0}{R_e} = \frac{1}{\alpha},\qquad r_e = \alpha R_e,\qquad a_e \approx \frac{\alpha}{2\pi}
```

Also the binding energy α²/2 and the mass ratios (section 5). For α the system is homogeneous: the data measure one α with known factors, so there are three unknowns and two equations from data. c\_g = 1 is a calibration, like G for gravity; two numbers have to be derived — q and N\_e.

## 6. Motion of a form and the tick

A form is a constant pattern that shifts as a whole together with the flowing medium; there are no fractional ticks.

- **The placement of places is not fixed** \[derived, V-197\]. With fixed places the centre point changes every tick, but after a tick the same one returns and never leaves the form. This contradicts A-02 (there are no fixed points) and A-47 (every point passes through the whole). So the places flow together with the medium.
- **A form shifts as a whole, like a pattern** \[derived, V-198\]. No point carries it: the points of all places, including the centre, change every tick (A-57, A-58).
- **There are no separate "transfer ticks"** \[derived, V-198\]. Every act is a whole step with two components: transfer β and closure ι. β² + ι² = 1 is a decomposition of one step, not a division of the tick. The former wording "fraction of transfer ticks" gave β + ι = 1 and contradicted A-39; withdrawn.
- **All fractions are ratios of integer counts** \[derived, V-198\]. β, ι, 3/4, 2/α² are ratios of numbers of ticks or steps.
- **The figure-eight is the pattern itself, not the mass cycle** \[derived, V-203\]. The pattern is constant, it has nothing to repeat. The mass frequency is a property of what changes: points and links. By A-60 it is the rate difference with the global figure-eight (section 1).
- **Orbit** \[candidate, V-212\] — the number of ticks over which a particle lags behind its global figure-eight by one exchange; mass is the rate of this lag.

## 10. Quarks, the sign of charge and the W-boson link (25.11)

One rule — a direction in three-dimensional space is divided by squares into three axes of 1/3 each (A-39, A-31), and a quark is a fragment of k axes (A-15) — gives the charges, "colours" and contribution to the running of α for all particles of a generation (V-304, candidate). The link "the W boson does not screen" cannot yet be tested with data.

**Not "Lego"** \[candidate, V-304\]. The variants are equal states of one whole, each screens with its own share squared. Adding "parts" (square of the sum) would give 1 and 4 instead of 1/3 and 4/3 — excluded. Only whole directions (all axes or none) exist freely — a reading of quark confinement.

**The sign of the u quark and generations** \[candidate, V-305\]. The proton loops (+), (−), (+) (A-23) with charge +1 give a unique solution with fragments: u = +2/3 on the "+" loops, d = −1/3 on the "−" loop. Neutron (A-52): +2/3 − 1/3 + 2/3 − 1 = 0. W⁻ decays are the division of three axes into (3, 0): an electron and an antineutrino, and (1, 2): d and anti-u. There are three generations — point O in 3D holds at most three figure-eights (V-186).

**Baryons** \[candidate, V-306\]. Ω⁻ = sss (three "−" in a row) lives 8.2·10⁻¹¹ s and decays only weakly ([PDG](https://pdglive.lbl.gov/Particle.action?node=S024)); Ξcc⁺⁺ with charge +2 does too, 0.26 ps ([LHCb, PRL 121, 052002](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02744)). The rule "neighbouring same-sign loops are a contradiction" was fixed at the source for baryons (V-192, V-230): fragments of the same sign on different axes stand side by side. The three "colours" of a baryon are three different axes.

**The sign of charge in q₀** \[candidate, V-303\]. The sign comes from the direction of the loop (A-15), it has no direction in space (A-17); the unscreened fraction is the modulus of the pair remainder, q₀ = 0.123, the charge does not wander. A projection onto the direction to the observer would give 1/α = 661 at the Z distance — excluded.

**The W-boson link** \[open, V-299, V-301, V-302\]. The W boson exists (CERN, 1983). The full Standard Model including it describes 17 precise electroweak observables (p = 0.68) and predicts the W mass 80355.8 ± 6.1 MeV against the measured 80362.5 ± 7.7 ([Gfitter](https://project-gfitter.web.cern.ch/Standard_Model/), [PDG 2026](https://pdgweb.lbl.gov/2026/reviews/rpp2026-rev-w-mass.pdf)) — the structure must reproduce the same numbers. There is no direct test: without the W contribution there would be 3–6 % more high-mass lepton pairs at 0.5–2 TeV, while the CMS data uncertainties there are 12–160 % ([JHEP 12 (2019) 059](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.10529)). The weak length λ\_F, like the electron mass, is an absolute scale: in the current postulates it is calibration (A-14), so the W mass cannot yet be obtained from the structure. The result of section 9 (1/α ≈ 137) rests on this unverified link.
